Milonga Lo-fi

"With Milonga Lo-fi I open a new path in gaucho music: the fusion of traditional milonga and contemporary lo-fi."

Recorded outdoors in Herval in the winter of 2022, in an encounter between the guitar and the soundscape of the border with Uruguay, the album brings together nine tracks — about 18 minutes, best heard with headphones — that evoke the countryside and the elements that make up the sound of the eastern border gaucho, a drover with no gates. The sound layers were not recorded separately: the southern lapwing, the wind, the insects, the footsteps, the distance and the silence of the border shape the texture of the tracks as much as the chords.

The album was released on all streaming platforms on 20 September 2026, the date that marks the beginning of the Ragamuffin Revolution (Revolução Farroupilha) and is celebrated in Rio Grande do Sul as Gaucho Day — the track "Retorno Du Herval" engages directly with that founding episode. Distributed by Ditto Music.

Tracks
01
Paleteada
The milonga bass line played on electric guitar, connecting the world of the countryside to the digital.
02
Troteando
An atmosphere of arriving at a hybrid place — a kind of dream, with fairies, innocence and discovery.
03
Charla do Mate [Oração do Gaúcho]
The solitary scene of the gaucho and his mate, listening to the lapwings in the field and chatting with his soul.
04
Tom, o Cachorro Branco
The tender intimacy between the wandering gaucho and his companion Tom, who runs, disappears into the brush and comes back at a whistle.
05
Retorno Du Herval
A poem built from accounts by the generals who faced each other in the first battle of the Ragamuffin Revolution, in the fields of Herval.
06
Sol de Inverno
Dusk and all the meanings of a day that has ended. Animation drawing on the imagery of artist Cassiano Miranda.
07
Formiga de Asa
Gaucho folk wisdom: when rain is coming, winged ants appear — in the poem, a sign that the rain is over.
08
O Mundo das Milongas [Pegado nos Arreios]
An excerpt from the lyrics of singer-songwriter Mauro Moraes.
09
Alvorada
The urban, apartment-dwelling gaucho, far more digital than analogue — but still with the milonga bass line inside him.
